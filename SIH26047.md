### 3. The Mathematics & Scoring Algorithms 🧮

To prove technical depth to the jury, your backend cannot just use a generic LLM prompt. It must utilize hard mathematical scoring for both triage (Allopathic) and *Prakriti* (Ayush).

#### A. The *Prakriti* Dosha Indexing Formula

Based on the 2025 classical Ayurvedic assessment models, your system should calculate a weighted probability score for the three Doshas (Vata, Pitta, Kapha) based on the conversational entities extracted.
Let $V_i, P_i, K_i$ be the binary presence (1 or 0) of a trait (e.g., deep sleep = Kapha, strong appetite = Pitta) across $n$ questions, and $w_i$ be the clinical weight of that specific trait.

The scoring algorithm for Pitta dominance is calculated as:


$$Score_{pitta} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (w_i \cdot P_i)$$

If $Score_{pitta} > Score_{vata}$ and $Score_{pitta} > Score_{kapha}$, the dashboard flags the patient's probable constitution as "Pitta-Dominant" for the doctor to review.

#### B. The Information Gathering Efficacy (F1 Score)

To evaluate if your conversational agent successfully utilized the *SOCRATES* framework (Site, Onset, Character, Radiation, etc.), you can apply the *Note2Chat* metric for Information Retrieval:


$$Precision = \frac{\vert{}Elicited\ Findings\ \cap\ Target\ Findings\vert{}}{\vert{}Elicited\ Findings\vert{}}$$

$$Recall = \frac{\vert{}Elicited\ Findings\ \cap\ Target\ Findings\vert{}}{\vert{}Target\ Findings\vert{}}$$

This ensures your AI doesn't just chat indefinitely, but actively hunts for the 8 specific parameters of a pain complaint before stopping the interview.

### 2. Mechanics of the Multimodal Engine (Voice + Touch) 🎙️👆

* *If the patient speaks:* "My chest hurts." (ASR catches it).

4. **State Update:** The backend Large Language Model (LLM) registers "Site = Chest" and instantly moves to the next node in the clinical decision tree.

### 3. Adaptive Questioning: Mirroring Clinical Reasoning (SOCRATES) 🧠

* `Site`: Chest (Filled)
* `Onset`: Yesterday / Acute (Filled)
* `Character`: Null (Missing)
* `Radiation`: Null (Missing)


* **Dynamic Branching (LLM Action):** The LLM detects that "Character" and "Radiation" are missing for a high-risk site (Chest). It also hits the **Red-Flag Detection** trigger.
* **Next Output:** The LLM generates the next question: *"Does the pain feel like a heavy weight, and does it spread to your left arm or jaw?"*

* **Implementation:** We prompt-engineer the LLM strictly. It does not "chat" freely. It receives the patient's text, extracts entities (Site, Onset, Character), updates a JSON state tracker, and outputs the absolute best *next* question based on the SOCRATES framework.

#### D. The RAG Pipeline: Pinecone (Vector DB) + BGE-m3 Embeddings

* **Working:** Standard LLMs fail at Indian folk idioms (e.g., *"Pitta bhadak gaya"*). Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) acts as our semantic translator.
* **Implementation:**
1. We scrape the official **NAMASTE Portal** and WHO ICD-11 TM2 (Traditional Medicine) codebook into a CSV.
2. We convert these terms into vector embeddings using the multilingual `BGE-m3` model and store them in Pinecone.
3. When a patient uses a colloquial phrase, the system embeds it, does a similarity search in Pinecone, and retrieves the exact standardized Ayush clinical term before passing it to the doctor's summary.



#### E. Document OCR (Past Medical History): Google Cloud Document AI

* **Working:** To handle the patient's past medical history, we need to digitize physical records.
* **Implementation:** *Scope Hackathon Cut:* Do not attempt cursive handwriting OCR (it will fail). Restrict this to **Printed Lab Reports**. The patient holds a printed blood test up to the kiosk camera. Document AI extracts the tables, pulling key-value pairs (e.g., `HbA1c: 7.2`) and flagging abnormal values automatically.

## 1. Key Academic & Operational Research Papers## 📄 Paper A: "The use of a kiosk-model bilingual self-triage system in the Emergency Department"

* 
* Source/Context: Published across major emergency medicine domains (e.g., [National Institutes of Health / PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9898217/)). [1, 2] 
* Approach: This study evaluated self-service kiosks that collect primary demographic details and historical data (medical history, current medications, reason for attendance) using a dual-language interface (English and Spanish) with audio-assisted touch screen sequences. It was designed to offset the initial data-collection burden from triage nurses. [1, 2, 3] 
* Formulas & Metrics Used:
* Throughput Delta ($\Delta T$): Measures the average time saved per patient.
   $$\Delta T = T_{\text{nurse\_triage}} - T_{\text{kiosk\_triage}}$$ 
   Results: $T_{\text{nurse\_triage}}$ averaged 126.7 seconds, while $T_{\text{kiosk\_triage}}$ averaged 94.4 seconds. The kiosk completed intake roughly 25% faster than human triage.
   * Discrepancy Score ($S_d$): Calculated by matching kiosk logs directly against ultimate clinical diagnoses to track error margins ($P < 0.05$ showing fewer errors in the kiosk cohort). [1, 2] 
* Flaws & Gaps:
* The Literacy-Time Disconnect: The study proved a significant inverse correlation ($r = -0.26$) between patient education level and the time spent on the kiosk. Lower-literacy users struggled with the structured UI text loops, extending their completion time drastically.
   * Static Triage Chains: It relied on static, rigid branching paths. It lacked generative, clinical LLM-driven follow-ups to catch atypical secondary symptoms. [1, 2]

## 📄 Paper B: "Automated Interactive Patient History-Taking System: A Review"

* 
* Source/Context: Published in health information technology journals. [4] 
* Approach: A comprehensive evaluation of computer-assisted interactive systems designed to extract a patient’s "anamnesis" (medical history) prior to the physical consultation to optimize medical workflows. It focused on structuring historical symptom data into standard electronic formats (like SOAP/FHIR) to prevent adverse drug reactions and medical transcription errors. [4, 5, 6] 
* Formulas & Metrics Used:
* Clinical Completeness Index (CCI): Elicitation depth measured by the ratio of captured relevant clinical variables ($V_c$) against standard textbook history frameworks ($V_s$):
   $$\text{CCI} = \frac{\sum V_c}{\sum V_s} \times 100$$ 
   * Doctor Review Time Efficiency: The study noted that pre-captured automated records recovered 4 to 7 minutes of direct verbal history-taking per consultation slot. [7] 
* Flaws & Gaps:
* The "Clean Data" Assumption: These systems assume patients can provide clean, chronological data inputs. They provide no mechanism to ingest or parse chaotic, unstructured, handwritten paper prescriptions or historic lab reports from alternate medical systems.
   * Cultural & Language Monolith: Most architectures are tested in homogeneous environments, completely failing when introduced to multi-lingual code-switching (e.g., "Hinglish") or noisy, high-decibel hospital waiting rooms.

## 📄 Paper C: "CliniKiosk / HERMES: AI-Powered Public Health Automated Kiosk Systems"

* 
* Source/Context: Developed out of institutions like MIT (2025/2026). [8, 9] 
* Approach: This line of research introduces intelligent, empathetic kiosks using localized AI logic (such as Gated Attention Transformers and Multi-Head Cross-Attention networks) to deliver real-time, culturally-adaptive medical questionnaires and point-of-care recommendations. [8, 9] 
* Formulas & Metrics Used:
* Federated Privacy Utility Matrix: Computes safety and cross-site analytical accuracy without sending raw data off the kiosk, utilizing secure distributed learning parameters.
   * Drug-Drug Interaction (DDI) Risks: Algorithmic calculation evaluating pre-existing patient medications against newly stated symptoms to warn doctors of diagnostic anomalies. [8] 
* Flaws & Gaps:
* Over-Sophistication vs. Bedside Reality: These platforms emphasize deep conversational pathways and localized micro-diagnostics. In an Indian government OPD setting with 5,000+ daily patients, long conversational sessions create a secondary bottleneck right at the kiosk line.
   * Ecosystem Disconnect: They operate as high-tech silos. They do not natively bridge structural mappings to localized government digital health standard architectures (like India’s ABDM/ABHA FHIR rails).
